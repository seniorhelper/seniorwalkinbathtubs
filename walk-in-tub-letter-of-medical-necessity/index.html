Letter of Medical Necessity for a Walk-In Tub, Done Right

Build a clear draft your clinician can edit, sign and date.

Get a free quoteCall 1-888-779-2284

A letter of medical necessity for a walk-in tub is a short, signed statement from a clinician that names your functional limit, explains why your current tub is unsafe, lists what you already tried and specifies the features you need. HSA, FSA, waiver and insurance reviewers ask for it. For 2027, an HSA can take up to $4,500 single or $9,000 family.

Updated November 17, 2026 by Zach Wennstedt, Aging Safely Baths. 9 minute read.

In short

  • The letter is written and signed by your clinician. You can hand them a draft, but the words and the judgment must be theirs.
  • Function beats diagnosis. "Cannot lift the right leg above 10 inches" carries more weight than "osteoarthritis."
  • Date it before you buy when you can, and keep it with the itemized invoice for at least as long as you keep your tax records.
  • 2027 limits: HSA $4,500 self-only or $9,000 family (plus $1,000 at 55 and older), health FSA $3,500 with up to $700 carryover.
  • Only the medical-care portion counts: the cost of the tub minus any increase in your home's value, under IRS Publication 502.

Who asks for a letter of medical necessity for a walk-in tub?

Almost everyone who might help pay. The letter is the bridge between "a nicer bathtub" and "medical care," and each reviewer reads it with a different question in mind. An HSA administrator wants to know whether the expense meets the IRS definition of medical care. A Medicaid case manager wants to know whether it fits a waiver service. The IRS, if it ever asks, wants to know why the improvement was needed.

Knowing who will read the letter tells you what to emphasize. Here is how the main readers use it.

Who reads a walk-in tub letter of medical necessity, and why
ReaderWhat they are checkingWhat to include
HSA administratorWhether the expense is medical care under Internal Revenue Code section 213(d)The letter, the itemized invoice, and a note on any change in home value
Health FSA administratorThe same 213(d) test, plus your plan's own claim rules and deadlinesThe letter dated within the plan year, the invoice and the claim form
Medicaid waiver case managerWhether the tub fits the state's home modification service and capThe program's own assessment form; your letter supports it but rarely replaces it
VA (HISA grant)Whether the change is medically needed for a veteran's conditionThe VA clinician's prescription; HISA does not pay for spa or jet features
Medicare Advantage planWhether a supplemental home-safety benefit applies, and prior approvalThe plan's request form, the letter and the itemized quote
IRS, at auditWhy the improvement was medically needed and how the amount was figuredKeep the letter, invoice and any appraisal with your return records

Programs differ on one detail that trips families up: some pay a contractor directly after approval, and some only reimburse. Either way, the letter has to exist before anyone pays. For the payer-by-payer picture, see our guide to which insurance and programs cover walk-in tubs.

What separates a weak letter from a strong one?

Reviewers see a lot of one-line notes on prescription pads. Those are easy to deny because they say what the patient wants, not what the patient cannot do. A strong letter is specific enough that a stranger could picture the person trying to get into the old tub.

The table shows the same idea written two ways. The right column is the kind of detail a clinician can confirm from an exam.

Weak vs. strong wording in a walk-in tub letter
Part of the letterWeakStrong
Condition"Patient has arthritis.""Severe bilateral knee osteoarthritis with limited flexion; unable to lower to or rise from a floor-level seat without assistance."
The barrier"Has trouble with the tub.""Cannot safely clear a 15-inch tub wall; reports two near-falls during tub transfers in the past six months."
What was tried(left out)"Grab bars and a transfer bench were installed in 2025. Patient still cannot bathe without standing on a wet surface."
The request"Needs a walk-in tub.""Requires a walk-in tub with a low step-in threshold, a seat at chair height, grab bars and a thermostatic anti-scald valve."
Duration(left out)"The condition is permanent and expected to progress."

Two more habits help. Keep comfort words out of it: "relaxing," "spa" and "jets" make a reviewer think of luxury, and for the VA they point straight at features HISA will not fund. And match the letter to the paperwork you send with it. If the letter asks for a heated seat but the invoice shows a jetted deluxe model, expect a follow-up question about the difference.

What changes for HSA and FSA money in 2027?

The limits go up slightly on January 1. The IRS set the 2027 HSA limits in Revenue Procedure 2026-24 and announced the 2027 health FSA limits this fall. If you are deciding whether to pay for a tub in December or January, these numbers matter more than they look.

Account limits for paying a walk-in tub with pre-tax money
Account20262027
HSA, self-only coverage$4,400$4,500
HSA, family coverage$8,750$9,000
HSA catch-up, age 55 and older$1,000$1,000
Health FSA salary reduction$3,400$3,500
Health FSA carryover maximum (if the plan allows)$680$700

What that means in practice:

  • HSA money does not expire. You can pay for a tub from a balance built over several years, so the yearly limit only caps new deposits, not what you can spend.
  • Medicare changes the HSA, it does not empty it. Once you enroll in Medicare you cannot contribute, but you can keep spending what is already in the account on qualified medical expenses.
  • FSA money is use it or lose it. A plan may let you carry over up to $680 of 2026 money into 2027, or give a grace period, but not both. If you have a 2026 balance and a letter in hand, check your plan's deadline before December 31.
  • A couple with family HSA coverage and a catch-up for one spouse could deposit $10,000 for 2027. That still covers only part of a typical installed walk-in tub, so most families combine accounts, savings and any program they qualify for.

The tax side follows the same rules. IRS Publication 502 counts a medical home improvement only to the extent its cost exceeds the increase in your home's value, and lists bathroom modifications among the changes that generally do not add value. When should the letter be written and dated?

Before the purchase, if you can manage it. A letter dated after the tub is installed reads like paperwork created to fit an expense, and some FSA administrators will not accept it. The order that causes the fewest problems:

  1. Book the appointment first. Ask for an occupational or physical therapy home safety evaluation if the doctor wants support for the letter.
  2. Get the itemized quote and spec sheet. The clinician can then name features the chosen tub actually has.
  3. Get the signed, dated letter. On letterhead, with credentials and contact information.
  4. Ask any program for approval in writing before you schedule, if that program needs to pay.
  5. Buy, install and file. Submit the claim with the letter and invoice, and keep copies of both.

How long the letter stays good depends on the reader. Many administrators treat a letter as valid for the plan year it covers, and a permanent condition may only need a single letter. Frequently asked questions

Who can write a letter of medical necessity for a walk-in tub?

Usually the physician who treats the condition. Many HSA and FSA administrators also accept a nurse practitioner or physician assistant, and an occupational or physical therapist's home safety assessment makes a strong attachment. Medicaid waivers and the VA use their own clinicians and forms, so ask the program first.

Does a letter of medical necessity guarantee my HSA or FSA will pay?

No. The letter documents medical need, but the account administrator and IRS rules decide. Only the cost that counts as medical care qualifies, which for a home improvement means the cost minus any increase in the home's value.

Should the letter be dated before I buy the walk-in tub?

Yes, when you can. Many administrators look for a letter dated before or at the time of the purchase, and a letter written months later invites questions. Ask your plan how it handles letters dated after the expense.

What are the 2027 HSA and FSA limits?

For 2027 the HSA contribution limit is $4,500 for self-only coverage and $9,000 for family coverage, plus a $1,000 catch-up at 55 and older. The health FSA salary reduction limit is $3,500, with a maximum carryover of $700 in plans that allow it.

Can I use HSA money for a walk-in tub after I join Medicare?

Once you enroll in Medicare you can no longer contribute to an HSA, but IRS Publication 969 lets you keep spending money already in the account on qualified medical expenses, which can include the medical-care portion of a walk-in tub.
