Who buys a walk-in tub online, and why

Most walk-in tub companies will only sell you a tub if their own crew installs it. That model works for some people, but it leaves a lot of families out. We hear from these buyers every week:

  • Homeowners with a plumber they already trust. They want the tub, not a second contractor.
  • Rural families. Big installation companies often will not drive to their address.
  • Canadian homeowners. Most U.S. walk-in tub brands do not install north of the border.
  • Contractors and remodelers installing accessible bathrooms for clients.
  • Care homes, group homes and property managers upgrading more than one bathroom.
  • Adult children coordinating a project for a parent in another state.

Buying online with DIY delivery gives all of them access to quality senior walk-in tubs, with a specialist to answer questions, without being forced into one installer.

Shipping walk-in tubs to Canada

We ship walk-in tubs to homeowners and contractors across Canada. That matters, because Canadian seniors face the same bathroom risks American seniors do. The Public Health Agency of Canada reports that 20% to 30% of seniors fall each year, that falls cause 85% of seniors’ injury-related hospitalizations, and that half of the falls that lead to hospitalization happen at home.

What Canadian buyers should know

  • Ask for the full delivered price. Freight to your address, customs brokerage and any duties or taxes should be clear before you order.
  • Hire a licensed local plumber. Walk-in tub installation involves plumbing connections and, for jetted tubs, electrical work. Use licensed trades and follow local codes.
  • Check electrical requirements. Jetted tubs and in-line heaters need a dedicated circuit that meets Canadian electrical code.
  • Keep every invoice. Canada’s federal Home Accessibility Tax Credit lets an eligible senior, or an eligible family member supporting them, claim up to $20,000 in qualifying renovation expenses per year, for a credit of up to $3,000. The Canada Revenue Agency requires documents that identify the vendor, describe the goods or work and show the amount paid.
  • Look at provincial programs too. Some provinces offer their own seniors’ home-safety or accessibility programs, and the medical expense tax credit may also apply to some expenses.

Choosing the right tub when you are buying online

Buying online means you will not sit in a showroom tub first. Here is how to choose well anyway.

Start with how the bather moves

  • Walks with little or no help: a standard inward- or outward-swing walk-in tub.
  • Uses a walker or cane and struggles with steps: an outward-swing door with a low threshold and a wide opening.
  • Uses a wheelchair or transfers from a seat: a wheelchair transfer tub with an L-shaped outward door.
  • Larger or taller bather: a bariatric or extra-long tub with a wider seat and more interior room.

Then match the space

  • Standard 60-inch tub alcove: most 52- to 60-inch walk-in tubs will fit, with extension panels filling any gap.
  • Small bathroom or shower stall: look at compact walk-in tubs such as Ella’s 26-by-45-inch Mobile or 32-by-40-inch Front Entry.
  • Large bathroom: a lay-down or two-seat tub becomes possible.

Then decide on comfort features

  • Soaking only for the simplest, most affordable option.
  • Air jets, water jets or dual systems for massage. See our hydrotherapy guide.
  • Heated backrest or seat to stay warm while the tub fills and drains.
  • Dual drains to shorten the wait before you can open the door.

Understanding freight delivery

A walk-in tub is too big and heavy for ordinary parcel carriers, so it travels by freight. If you have never received a freight delivery, here is what to expect.

  • Scheduling. The freight carrier usually calls ahead to set a delivery window. Make sure the phone number on your order is one you answer.
  • Where it is dropped. Many freight deliveries are to the curb or the end of the driveway rather than inside the home. Ask us what service level your quote includes, and plan helpers accordingly.
  • Liftgate trucks. Residential deliveries often use a truck with a liftgate to lower the crate to the ground. Addresses on narrow or steep roads may need special arrangements.
  • Inspection. Open or look through the packaging before the driver leaves. If anything is damaged, write it on the delivery receipt before signing. This is the single most important step for resolving a shipping claim.
  • Moving it inside. Most tubs need two to four adults to carry safely. Measure every doorway, hall and stair on the way to the bathroom before delivery day.

A printable checklist for your installer

Hand this list to your plumber or contractor before the tub arrives.

  1. Read the manufacturer’s installation manual start to finish.
  2. Confirm the drain location matches the tub’s drain side and rough-in dimensions.
  3. Confirm supply line size and water pressure for the fast-fill faucet.
  4. For jetted or heated tubs, install a dedicated GFCI-protected circuit to the manufacturer’s amperage and to local code.
  5. Check that the subfloor is solid and level, and repair any water damage.
  6. Set the tub and level it precisely in both directions.
  7. Connect the drain, overflow, supply lines and any therapy system.
  8. Install extension panels or a wall kit and seal all seams.
  9. Fill the tub completely and check the door seal along its full length.
  10. Drain fully, confirm no standing water, and run every jet, heater and control.
  11. Walk the owner through the door latch, faucet, handheld shower, jets and drain.

Sizing worksheet: write these down before you call

Having these numbers ready makes your consultation fast and your recommendation accurate.

  • Alcove length, measured at the floor, middle and top of the tub area (use the smallest).
  • Alcove width, measured the same way.
  • Height from the floor to the bottom of any window or shelf above the tub.
  • Which side the faucet and drain are on as you face the tub.
  • Width of the bathroom door and any tight hallway or stair turns.
  • Water heater tank size in gallons, and gas or electric.
  • Whether the bathroom has an unused electrical outlet or circuit nearby.

Door styles explained for online buyers

The door is the heart of a walk-in tub, and it is the first choice to get right when you are buying without a showroom visit.

Inward-swinging doors

The door swings into the tub. Because it does not need clearance in the bathroom, it suits tight spaces where a toilet or vanity sits close to the tub. The trade-off is that the door takes up some space inside the tub as you enter, and water pressure helps hold it closed during the bath.

Outward-swinging doors

The door swings out into the bathroom, which leaves the full opening clear to enter. Outward doors are easier for people who use a walker or need a wider path, and they are standard on most wheelchair transfer tubs. You need enough floor space in front of the tub for the door to open fully.

L-shaped transfer doors

Transfer doors wrap around the corner of the tub and open very wide, so a wheelchair user can pull alongside and slide across onto the seat. Ella’s Transfer60, for example, uses a 36-inch L-shaped outward door with a 22-inch-wide seat.

Front-entry doors

A few compact models place the door on the short end of the tub, which can solve very narrow bathrooms. Ella’s Front Entry, at 32 by 40 inches, is one example.

Will the tub fit through my house?

This is the question that causes the most trouble for online buyers, so measure before you order.

  • Measure the narrowest point on the path: often the bathroom door, a hallway corner or a stair landing.
  • Compare that to the tub’s packaged depth and height, not just its length. A tub can often be tilted on its side through a doorway.
  • Remember door stops and trim reduce the clear opening. Measure between the narrowest points.
  • If the path is tight, removing a door from its hinges often gains an inch or more.

Send us your measurements and we will check them against the tub’s shipping dimensions before you order.

Spec-sheet terms in plain English

Product listings use terms that are not always obvious. Here is what they mean.

  • Threshold or step-in height: how high you lift your foot to enter. Lower is easier.
  • Seat height: how high the seat is from the tub floor. Chair height makes sitting and standing easier.
  • Seat width: the usable width of the seat. Bariatric bathers need more.
  • Water depth: how deep the water gets when you are seated. Deeper means a fuller soak.
  • Gallon capacity: how much water the tub holds. Check this against your water heater.
  • Fast-fill faucet: a high-flow faucet that shortens the fill time.
  • Dual drain: two drains, or a larger drain system, to shorten the wait before you can open the door.
  • Inline heater: keeps jet-circulated water warm during the bath.
  • Anti-scald or thermostatic valve: limits maximum water temperature.

Buying a walk-in tub for a parent who lives far away

Many online orders come from adult children coordinating a tub for a parent in another state or province. A few things make long-distance projects go smoothly:

  • Get the measurements from someone on site. A neighbor, a local relative or the plumber who will install the tub can send photos and numbers. Photos of the tub, the faucet wall, the bathroom door and the water heater label answer most of our questions.
  • Line up the installer first. Ask a local licensed plumber whether they will install a walk-in tub, and share our spec sheets with them before you order.
  • Choose a delivery day when someone can be there. Freight carriers need a person to sign and inspect the delivery.
  • Plan the walkthrough. Ask the installer to show your parent how to use the door, faucet and drain, or join by video call so you can help later.
  • Keep the paperwork together. Invoices, the warranty and the manual should live in one folder at your parent’s home and one copy with you.

We are glad to speak with both you and your parent, together or separately, so everyone is comfortable with the choice.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy a walk-in tub online and have it shipped to my house?

Yes. We drop-ship walk-in tubs to homes and jobsites anywhere in the United States and Canada. We provide spec sheets and rough-in dimensions so your installer can prepare before the tub arrives.

Do you ship walk-in tubs to Canada?

Yes. We ship to homeowners and contractors across Canada. Ask for freight, brokerage and any duty costs when you request a quote so the full delivered price is clear.

Can I install a walk-in tub myself?

A handy homeowner can do some of the work, but walk-in tub installation involves plumbing, precise leveling for a watertight door seal and often electrical work for jets or heaters. We recommend a licensed plumber and electrician, especially for jetted tubs.

What does my installer need from you?

The spec sheet, rough-in dimensions for the drain and supply lines, electrical requirements for any jets or heaters, and the installation manual. We send these before the tub ships.

Is there a warranty if I install it myself?

The manufacturer’s warranty applies, and many manufacturers require proper installation to keep it valid. Aging Safely Baths also provides a 1-year labor warranty on supply-only orders. Ask us for the written terms with your quote.

Can contractors and care facilities order through you?

Yes. Contractors, remodelers, care homes, group homes and property managers order walk-in tubs and roll-in showers from us for their projects.

How is a walk-in tub delivered?

Walk-in tubs ship by freight because of their size and weight. Plan to have helpers available to move the tub inside, and inspect it before signing the delivery receipt.

Can I claim a tax credit on a walk-in tub in Canada?

Canada’s Home Accessibility Tax Credit allows eligible individuals to claim up to $20,000 of qualifying renovation expenses per year for a senior or a person eligible for the disability tax credit, for a credit of up to $3,000. Walk-in bathtubs are commonly listed as eligible. Confirm your situation with the CRA or a tax professional.
