What happens after you send the form

We designed the quote process to be quick for you and accurate for your bathroom. Here is exactly what happens.

  1. You send the form or call 1-888-779-2284. Name, phone, ZIP or postal code and whether you want installation or delivery are all we need to start.
  2. A specialist calls you back. We confirm who will use the tub, any mobility needs, and the basics of the bathroom.
  3. We check your address. For installation, we confirm one-day installation availability for your ZIP code. For delivery, we confirm freight to your address, including Canada.
  4. We confirm measurements. Using your numbers or photos, we confirm the tub space, doorways, water heater and electrical.
  5. We recommend two or three tubs. Different sizes, door styles or therapy options, with the pros and cons of each.
  6. You receive a written quote. Tub, options, installation or freight, warranty and any likely extra work, all itemized.
  7. You decide when you are ready. Share it with your family, compare it with other quotes and call us with questions. There is no deadline.

Information that makes your quote faster and more accurate

You can request a quote with only your name, phone and ZIP code. But the more we know, the more precise the first recommendation will be. If you have them, these details help:

  • Tub or shower space: length and width, measured at the floor, middle and top.
  • Faucet and drain side: left or right as you face the tub.
  • Bathroom door width and any tight hallway turns.
  • Water heater: tank size in gallons, gas or electric.
  • Electrical: whether there is an outlet or spare circuit near the bathroom, if you want jets.
  • The bather: height, and whether they use a cane, walker or wheelchair.
  • Photos: the tub from the doorway, the faucet wall and the water heater label.
  • Any therapist recommendations, especially for wheelchair transfers.

Our measuring guide shows exactly where to put the tape measure, and our water heater guide has a quick calculator.

How to compare walk-in tub quotes

Whether you get one quote or five, compare them the same way.

  1. Compare the same tub type. A soaking tub and a dual-jet tub are not the same product.
  2. Check the door direction and threshold. These decide how easy the tub is to use every day.
  3. Check the seat width and height. Especially for larger or taller bathers.
  4. Check what installation includes. Removal, plumbing, electrical, wall finish, testing and cleanup.
  5. Check possible extra costs. Subfloor repair, drain relocation and electrical work should be addressed up front.
  6. Compare warranties. What is covered, for how long, and whether labor is included.
  7. Check who installs. Factory-certified installers who follow the manufacturer’s specifications protect your warranty.
  8. Watch for pressure tactics. Same-day-only prices are a red flag. A fair offer is still fair tomorrow.

Quotes for Canadian homeowners

We quote walk-in tubs for delivery anywhere in Canada. Your quote should include the tub, options and freight to your address, plus customs brokerage and any duties or taxes so the delivered price is complete. Keep your invoice: the federal Home Accessibility Tax Credit lets eligible Canadians claim up to $20,000 of qualifying renovation expenses per year for a senior or a person eligible for the disability tax credit, for a credit of up to $3,000. The CRA requires invoices that identify the vendor, describe the goods or work, and show the amount and payment.

What changes the number on a quote

Two families can choose the same tub and receive different quotes. Here is why, using common situations we see every week.

The straightforward swap. An existing 60-inch tub, drain on the correct side, sound floor, a 50-gallon water heater and a soaking tub with no jets. This is the simplest installation, and the quote is mostly the tub and standard labor.

The jetted upgrade. The same bathroom, but the family chooses a dual-jet tub with a heated backrest. Now the quote adds the therapy system and a dedicated GFCI circuit. If the electrical panel is full or far away, that line grows.

The hidden damage. A tub-for-tub swap where the old tub has leaked for years. When the tub comes out, the subfloor is soft. Our quote lists this possibility in advance with a range, so it is not a surprise, and the repair is done before the new tub goes in.

The shower conversion. The home has only a stall shower, and the family wants a compact walk-in tub. Moving the drain, adding a tub filler and finishing the walls adds labor and may add a second day.

The large tub and small heater. A two-seat tub in a home with a 40-gallon water heater. Either the tub choice changes, or the quote adds a larger or tankless water heater, or an in-line heater.

The long-distance delivery. A DIY order to a rural address or to Canada. The quote shows freight to the door, plus brokerage and duty for Canada, and no installation labor, since the family’s own plumber installs it.

Choosing between the models in your quote

We usually recommend two or three tubs. Here is how to choose between them.

  • Start with safe entry. The door style and threshold height should match how the bather moves today and in a few years.
  • Then check fit. Seat width, knee room and interior length should suit the bather’s body.
  • Then decide on comfort. Soaking only, air jets, water jets or both, plus heated backrest.
  • Then check the practical side. Water heater capacity, electrical needs and how the tub fits the bathroom.
  • Finally compare warranties. Especially on door seals, motors and heaters.

If two options feel equally good, choose the one with the better warranty and the simpler controls.

Quote mistakes to avoid

  • Comparing a soaking tub quote to a jetted tub quote. Make sure you are comparing the same kind of tub.
  • Ignoring the door direction. A tub that is cheaper but opens the wrong way for your bathroom is not a bargain.
  • Skipping the water heater question. A tub your heater cannot fill will leave you with lukewarm baths or a second purchase.
  • Accepting a lump sum. Ask for the tub, options and labor separately.
  • Not asking about extra work. Subfloor, drain and electrical questions should be answered before you sign.
  • Deciding under pressure. If a price is only good today, walk away and compare.
  • Forgetting the warranty. Compare what is covered and for how long, including labor.

Understanding delivery times

Walk-in tubs are large, specialized products, and availability changes during the year. Some popular models ship quickly; others are built or configured to order. Your quote will include a realistic delivery estimate for the model you choose. If timing is critical, tell us, and we will point you to models and options that can arrive sooner. It is better to know the true timeline up front than to be promised a date that cannot be met.

Canadian quote questions

Canadian customers often ask a few extra questions when they request a quote.

  • Is the price in U.S. or Canadian dollars? Your written quote will state the currency clearly.
  • Who handles customs? Ask us how brokerage is handled for your order and what it will cost, so the delivered price is complete.
  • Are duties or taxes charged at the border? Your quote should address this so there are no surprises at delivery.
  • Can I claim the Home Accessibility Tax Credit? Walk-in bathtubs are commonly listed as eligible, and our invoice includes the details the CRA asks for. Confirm your situation with a tax professional.
  • Who installs it? A local licensed plumber, and an electrician if the tub has jets or a heater. We send them the spec sheets.

What your quote will not include

Just as important as what a quote contains is what it leaves out. Our quotes will never include a surprise “processing fee,” a “today only” discount that disappears tomorrow, or a financing pitch you did not ask for. They also will not include products you do not need. If a feature is optional, the quote says so, and you can remove it. If a less expensive model fits your bathroom and meets the bather’s needs, we will show it to you side by side with the more expensive option, and explain the real difference between them in plain language.

Getting a second opinion

If you already have a quote from another company, you are welcome to send it to us. We will tell you honestly whether it looks fair for the tub and work described, point out anything that seems missing, and quote a comparable option if we carry one. Sometimes the best advice we can give is that the quote you already have is a good one.

Why the numbers matter to families

Families often ask whether a walk-in tub is worth the expense. The data on bathroom safety is part of the answer. In the CDC’s study of bathroom injuries treated in U.S. emergency rooms, injury rates rose sharply with age, reaching 515 per 100,000 among adults 85 and older, and most injuries were caused by falls.

The rest of the answer is independence. AARP’s 2024 survey found that 75% of adults 50 and older want to stay in their current homes as they age. A safe way to bathe is one of the most important parts of making that possible.

What we will ask you on the call

So there are no surprises, here are the questions a specialist will usually ask when we call you back. You do not need perfect answers; approximate ones are fine.

  • Who will use the tub, and roughly how tall are they?
  • What is the hardest part of bathing today: stepping in, sitting down, standing up or getting out?
  • Is a cane, walker or wheelchair used, now or likely in the next few years?
  • Is the tub going where a tub is now, or where a shower is now?
  • Is the bathroom on the main floor or upstairs?
  • Do you want jets, or is a deep, warm soak enough?
  • How big is your water heater?
  • Is there a timeline, such as a hospital discharge or a visit from family?
  • Are you applying to any funding programs that need paperwork?
  • Would you like installation, delivery, or help deciding?

These answers let us narrow dozens of possible models to the two or three that actually make sense for your home. Most calls take ten to fifteen minutes.
