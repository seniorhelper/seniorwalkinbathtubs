Is a walk-in tub tax deductible? Estimate yours in a minute

Often, yes, when the tub is for a medical need. The federal rules are in IRS Publication 502. Below is what they say, in their own words, and a calculator that applies them to your numbers.

Short answer: Under IRS Publication 502, a home improvement whose main purpose is medical care counts as a medical expense, reduced by any increase in your home’s value. The IRS lists “Installing railings, support bars, or other modifications to bathrooms” among changes that generally don’t increase home value, so the full cost often counts. You only benefit if you itemize, and only medical expenses above 7.5% of your adjusted gross income are deductible.

What IRS Publication 502 actually says

“The cost of the improvement is reduced by the increase in the value of your property.”
“If the value of your property isn’t increased by the improvement, the entire cost is included as a medical expense.”
Among improvements that generally don’t increase home value: “Installing railings, support bars, or other modifications to bathrooms” and “Adding handrails or grab bars anywhere (whether or not in bathrooms).”
You can deduct “only the part of your medical and dental expenses that is more than 7.5% of your adjusted gross income (AGI).”
“You can include medical expenses you paid for your dependent.”

Source: IRS, Publication 502 (2025), Medical and Dental Expenses.

Before you count it: a short checklist

  1. Medical purpose. The improvement’s main purpose should be medical care. A note from the doctor about why a step-in tub is needed is worth keeping with your records.
  2. Keep the paperwork. The itemized invoice for the tub and the installation, and proof of payment.
  3. Home value. If you think the tub raised your home’s value, an appraisal shows by how much. Only the cost above that increase counts.
  4. Who paid. If an adult child pays for a parent’s tub, it can count only if the parent qualifies as the child’s dependent under the Pub 502 rules.
  5. Itemize or not. Add the result to your other itemized deductions and compare with your standard deduction.

Other ways families pay, such as VA grants, Medicaid waivers and Medicare Advantage extras, are covered in Medicare, grants and tax credits. For price ranges, see walk-in tub cost.
